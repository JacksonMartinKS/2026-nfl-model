Order
Game quality (0 to 100) blends how close the game should be (45%), how good both teams are (47%) and both starting quarterbacks (8%). Team and QB scores use a harmonic mean, so one great team facing a bad one doesn't make a great game.
Leverage comes from the season simulations: every team's odds in seasons where the home team wins are compared with seasons where the away team wins, and the probability that changes hands across all 32 teams is added up, with simulation noise stripped out. Playoff odds count 60%, division titles 20%, #1 seeds 10% and Super Bowl odds 10%, each scaled by how many there are (14 playoff spots, 8 division titles, 2 #1 seeds, 1 Super Bowl), so the result reads in playoff-odds points.
Elo is each team's rating going into the week. Rest adjusts for days since the last game (short week or extra rest), Field is home-field advantage (smaller at neutral sites), QB adjusts for the starting quarterback, and Travel for distance traveled. Game Elo adds them up, and the gap between the two Game Elos sets the predicted margin and win chance. GQI (game quality index, 0–100) rates how strong the matchup is. The tier under each team uses its Elo with the QB adjustment: . Past weeks show each game as forecast before kickoff, with the final score and whether the favorite won.

Week by week

Each game's winner follows the model's odds, using the same ratings, home field, rest, travel, QB adjustments and season-long uncertainty in team strength as the forecast. Scores are built drive by drive: about 11 drives a side, each ending in a touchdown (usually 7, sometimes a missed extra point or a 2-point try), a field goal or nothing, plus the odd safety. How many points a team scores and allows comes from splitting its rating into offense and defense using the points it has scored and allowed this season (pulled toward an even split early on) and its starting QB. Tied games go to overtime. This is one random season; the Projections tab shows the averages over thousands of them.

    How the forecast works

    Every game is decided by an Elo rating model. Ratings carry over from last season with partial reversion to the mean, update after each result based on margin of victory, and adjust each game for home field, rest, travel distance, and the starting quarterback.

    After each week, the rest of the schedule is simulated many times with random variation in every team's true strength. Each percentage is the share of simulated seasons where that outcome happened, and projected wins include games already played.
